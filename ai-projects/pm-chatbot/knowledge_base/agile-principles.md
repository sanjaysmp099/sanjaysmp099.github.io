---
title: Agile values and principles
source_url: https://agilemanifesto.org/
---

Agile is a family of approaches to software and product development that
emerged in the early 2000s as a reaction against heavyweight, plan-driven
processes. The Agile movement is best known for a short set of values that
express what its practitioners came to prioritize over time.

At a high level, Agile values favor: individuals and interactions over rigid
processes and tooling; working software (or a working product) over
exhaustive documentation; collaborating with customers over negotiating
contract terms; and responding to change over rigidly following a plan. The
point is not that the second item in each pair has no value, but that the
first is valued more highly when the two are in tension.

From these values, a set of guiding principles follows. Agile teams aim to
satisfy customers through early and continuous delivery of valuable work.
They welcome changing requirements, even late in a project, treating change
as a competitive advantage rather than a disruption. They favor frequent
delivery of working results, often every few weeks. Business people and
developers are expected to work together daily throughout the project.
Motivated individuals are given the environment and trust they need to get
work done. Face-to-face conversation is treated as the most effective way to
convey information within a team. Progress is measured primarily by working
output, not by plans or paperwork. Agile also emphasizes sustainable pace,
technical excellence, simplicity (maximizing the amount of work not done),
self-organizing teams, and regular reflection on how to become more
effective, with the team adjusting its behavior accordingly.

Agile itself is not a single method — Scrum, Kanban, and Extreme Programming
(XP) are all specific frameworks that embody these values and principles in
different ways.
