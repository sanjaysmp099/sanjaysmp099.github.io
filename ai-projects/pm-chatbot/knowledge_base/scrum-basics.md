title: Scrum framework basics
source_url: https://scrumguides.org/

Scrum is a lightweight framework for managing product development work, built
around short, fixed-length iterations called sprints, typically one to four
weeks long. Each sprint aims to produce a usable increment of the product.

Three accountabilities exist in Scrum. The Product Owner is responsible for
maximizing the value of the product and manages the product backlog, an
ordered list of everything that might be needed. The Scrum Master helps the
team follow Scrum practices and removes obstacles that slow progress. The
Developers are the people who do the actual work of building the increment.

Scrum defines four core events. Sprint Planning kicks off the sprint by
deciding what can be delivered and how the work will be done. The Daily
Scrum is a short daily check-in where the team synchronizes and plans the
next 24 hours. The Sprint Review happens at the end of the sprint to inspect
the increment and adapt the backlog based on feedback. The Sprint
Retrospective follows, giving the team a chance to reflect on how the sprint
went and identify improvements for the next one.

Scrum relies on three artifacts: the Product Backlog (all known work), the
Sprint Backlog (work selected for the current sprint plus a plan to deliver
it), and the Increment (the sum of completed backlog items, tested and
potentially releasable).

Scrum is not a methodology with detailed prescriptions — it is a minimal
framework. Teams are expected to layer their own practices, such as specific
estimation techniques or engineering practices, on top of it.
