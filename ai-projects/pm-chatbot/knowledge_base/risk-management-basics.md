---
title: Project risk management fundamentals
source_url: https://www.dau.edu/
---

Risk management in a project context is the ongoing process of identifying,
assessing, and responding to uncertainties that could affect a project's
objectives — whether that's cost, schedule, scope, or quality.

Risk identification is the first step: systematically listing things that
could go wrong (threats) or unexpected opportunities that could help
(upside risks). Common techniques include brainstorming with the team,
reviewing lessons learned from past projects, and checking assumptions and
constraints for hidden fragility.

Once identified, risks are usually assessed along two dimensions: how likely
they are to occur, and how severe the impact would be if they did. Plotting
risks on a simple probability-impact grid helps teams decide which risks
deserve active attention versus which can be monitored passively.

Response strategies generally fall into a few categories. For threats: avoid
(change the plan to remove the risk entirely), mitigate (reduce the
likelihood or impact), transfer (shift the risk to a third party, such as
through insurance or a contract clause), or accept (acknowledge the risk and
do nothing further, usually because it's low priority or unavoidable). For
opportunities, the mirrored strategies are exploit, enhance, share, and
accept.

A risk register — a living document listing each risk, its assessment, its
owner, and its planned response — is a standard tool for tracking risks
throughout a project's life, rather than treating risk management as a
one-time exercise done only at kickoff.
